# Playing a session

## Saying something

The composer at the bottom has three channels.

- **In character**: what your character says or does. Speech goes in quotes; actions are written as what
  your character does. The formatter sets each into its own block, credited to its speaker.
- **Out of character**: table talk. It never enters the story.
- **To the GM**: a question for the game master. The GM answers as commentary; a question about what someone
  in the scene is doing is answered by that person acting in the story.

## A round

After your line, the round runs on its own:

1. If your line is ambiguous (which one do you punch?), the GM asks, with the choices.
2. Anyone the line approaches who isn't in the world yet is authored first.
3. The fight check runs: if your line starts a fight, the VTT starts it.
4. If your line calls for a roll, the VTT rolls it and the narrator tells what happened.
5. The decision model picks who acts next: someone in the party, someone present, the GM, or you. It keeps
   going until it's your turn again.

While any of this runs, a line at the bottom says who is writing, or what is being worked on (generating,
formatting, deciding), and **Stop** is offered. Stop ends the whole round, not just the reply being written.
A reply stays on screen until its saved, formatted version replaces it.

## Banners

A banner marks a scene change in the VTT, and only that: when the GM moves the table to a new map or level.
It is its own record, never part of the narration.

## Turns that run on the server

A reply is written on the server. Leaving the tab, a dropped network or a reload doesn't lose it: the page
follows it again when it comes back, and the processing state tells the page what is still running.
